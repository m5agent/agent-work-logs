# BBPLL startup diagnosis and combined QEMU observations

## Original failure and single-change comparison

On the previous native QEMU executable eecf42ad4b393a76a9208c644abc2606030b585824b24ae53e5e9ecad2b4d4ed, the ESP-IDF v6.1.1 Cardputer fixture produced 2,648 console bytes without reaching app_main in 50 seconds. Live CPU snapshots showed CPU0 at the load ahead of the calibration polling branch, while CPU1 was waiting in ROM. The matching guest ELF placed CPU0 in clk_ll_bbpll_calibration_is_done, called from rtc_clk_bbpll_configure. The guest polls bit 24 at 0x6000e040. The old model had no completion status at that offset. This case remains a startup FAIL and a filesystem NOT RUN. [First failure and reference evidence](evidence/bbpll-6-1-source-summary.json)

Patch 0211 reports digital calibration completion when STOP_FORCE_LOW is set and STOP_FORCE_HIGH is clear. The rebuilt BBPLL-only native executable 21755b0c95f0456e80ad3a1242a86a0214ab0f8136bae08507dc2093ee6d095c ran the very same v6.1.1 merged Flash b8fe2d8a0baf3466b60800713cf9070e8f030f9e60f057151dd47ccce28496bf and LittleFS seed 7c6462fc7c499539df682815543e01e1c130a9d7caef5952f60c716ab71d9c81. The guest mounted, listed and read the seed, wrote and recovered the proof across remount, and observed the saved marker after reset. The v5.5.4 Cardputer, ADV and empty-baseline controls also passed on this single-patch runtime. [Exact model source](https://github.com/m5agent/m5stack-esp-emulator/blob/6da7ab20351d732c6738fe13311f686efc856db3/patches/qemu-m5stack-peripherals/0211-s3-bbpll-calibration-completion.patch)

## Combined runtime repeat

Integrated source 6da7ab20351d732c6738fe13311f686efc856db3 combines open SoftAP and the BBPLL status change. The final native executable SHA-256 is 5f8a5f38f0512db04a36fb7ca5cf13ec3d359d0fff1eb481fe9b2c06ab3580f2. The six-case LittleFS test harness used its pinned Device Service source 803e0a0500494d44e65586f8fc79e089b92ee1a0 with that exact combined executable. Each SDK version ran Cardputer with seed, Cardputer ADV with seed, and a Cardputer empty-volume control. All six passed. Seeded cases restored the saved file after reset. Empty volumes began without the seed. Case-specific Flash comparison preserved all bytes outside the target partition. All sessions released Flash and leases, and source artifacts remained unchanged. [Case, firmware and ZIP hashes](evidence/combined-littlefs-summary.json)

Core2 used the same combined executable on the final integrated Device Service for a separate ESP-IDF v5.5.4 watchdog probe. The repeat observed four early live samples and a CPU1 watchdog panic with a complete UART dump. The live post-panic PCs, 0x400d1b33 and 0x400814e6, are recorded separately from the guest panic-time values, 0x4000bfea and 0x400d7d19. The correct ELF matched, the mismatched ELF was excluded, and the ZIP passed CRC and serial hash checks. The first combined attempt hit the watchdog before the expected post-resume observation window and failed the timing assertion. Both attempts released the session and its Flash. [Repeat and first-attempt summary](evidence/combined-core2-watchdog-summary.json)

Cardputer ADV separately repeated the fixed open-AP reset fixture on the combined runtime. The capture recorded two DHCPv4 DORAs and two bidirectional TCP flows, with the peer released. That observation covers one ADV lease and the fixed fixture. [Combined SoftAP evidence](../../../2026-11-17-isolated-native-open-softap/evidence/combined-adv-reset-summary.json)

## Reproduction and limits

Build the pinned LittleFS fixtures and preserve the input images from the [original record](../../report.md). Run the v6.1.1 image and seed on the old executable, then on the BBPLL-only executable and the combined executable. Use independent leases, read the named UART markers, reset seeded sessions, compare Flash outside the partition and request ZIP evidence before stopping. On Core2, run the ESP-IDF watchdog fixture with the exact ELF, collect live PC samples and the UART panic in the same session, and test matching versus mismatched ELF decoding and the post-stop endpoint. CPU reads are sequential and the panic may race with a host sample.

The evidence covers firmware-visible digital status. Analog PLL timing, other ESP-IDF versions, production use, browser-WASM, RF, physical hardware and arbitrary captive portals are outside this record.
